Research behind the activity

This original Italian adaptation uses sentence–picture interpretation: read for meaning and choose a scene, without producing a pronoun. It is research-informed practice, not a tested replication or a complete Processing Instruction intervention.

VanPatten, B., & Cadierno, T. (1993). Explicit instruction and input processing. Studies in Second Language Acquisition, 15(2), 225–243. Motivates comprehension practice that changes how learners interpret input. Our actor contrasts draw attention to who performs the action; recipient contrasts require interpreting gli/le.

Wong, W., & VanPatten, B. (2003). The evidence is IN: Drills are OUT. Foreign Language Annals, 36(3), 403–423. Supports meaningful interpretation rather than form-only drills; consulted from the supplied article.

Design: direct objects first, indirect recipients second, then mixed review; immediate meaning-based feedback and optional repetition. Some Italian subjects follow the verb to contrast word orders; these are context-dependent focus constructions, not the only normal word order. Singular gli means “to him” here; other contemporary uses are outside this activity.

Illustrations are original AI-generated textbook-style line drawings. No third-party textbook artwork is reproduced.

Before you begin

Read the sentence and choose the matching picture. Names below the sentence identify the characters in both pictures. Striped shirts help you follow the same people. The subject and the person or people referred to by the pronoun always differ in gender or number.

Lo / la / li / le: him / her / them (masculine) / them (feminine). Gli / le: to him / to her. Abbraccia = hugs; saluta = greets; insegue = chases; fotografa = photographs; scrive = writes; telefona = phones.

The subject can follow the verb: «Lo abbraccia Giulia» means Giulia hugs him. Ask who acts, rather than treating the first person mentioned as the actor.
